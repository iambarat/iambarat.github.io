about

I'm a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at Virginia Tech, where I've been a graduate research assistant since 2022 and expect to graduate in the summer of 2027. My work sits at the point where cryptography meets real deployed systems: I design protocols that let people pay, use products, and prove things about themselves without giving up their privacy.

Lately that has meant a pay-as-you-go payment system for dynamic spectrum sharing, anonymous but still compliant fiat-to-crypto exchange, Sybil-resistant pseudonyms for connected vehicles, and attacks and defenses for the sensor fusion pipelines that self-driving cars depend on.

My recent works are focused on developing privacy-preserving charging mechanisms for EV, and agentic AI based approaches for spectrum allocation.

research

  • SpexPay

    A privacy-preserving pay-as-you-go payment system for dynamic spectrum sharing, letting users pay for spectrum access without revealing their usage patterns to the spectrum manager.

    Zero-knowledge proofs · Spectrum sharing · ESORICS 2026
    Paper Code

  • Anonymous yet compliant exchange

    An anonymous payment design for fiat-to-cryptocurrency exchange that still satisfies regulatory compliance requirements, so privacy and accountability don't have to be traded against each other.

    Anonymous payments · Compliance · IEEE INFOCOM 2026
    Paper

  • Temporal misalignment in sensor fusion

    Attacks that desynchronize the multimodal sensor streams autonomous vehicles fuse for perception, together with a detection method that catches the misalignment before it reaches the planner.

    ML security · Autonomous driving · IEEE SaTML 2026 · ICLR 2026
    Paper Code

  • V-PASS

    Sybil-resistant pseudonym self-provisioning for V2X, so vehicles can rotate identities for privacy without one vehicle being able to masquerade as many.

    V2X · Pseudonymity · ACM WiSec 2026
    Paper

  • SoK: Public blockchain sharding

    A systematization of knowledge that maps how public blockchains shard, what security each approach assumes, and where the open problems in scalability remain.

    Blockchain · Scalability · IEEE ICBC 2024
    Paper

  • Privacy-preserving EV charging

    An EV charging protocol with anonymous payment, so drivers can charge without building a location history tied to their identity. Under submission.

    Anonymous payments · Electric vehicles
